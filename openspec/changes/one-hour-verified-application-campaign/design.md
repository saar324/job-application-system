## Context and measured constraints

On 23 September, ten owner-approved roles yielded seven new receipts and three previously submitted roles. The clock from first application record to seventh receipt was 196.75 minutes. The interval before batch approval was about 136.05 minutes and the interval after it was 60.70 minutes; these are coarse wall-clock blocks, not clean preparation and browser samples. Headless Ashby submissions failed or became uncertain while ordinary Chrome completed five Ashby forms. The final 50-source replacement scan accepted no candidate. The previous pilot showed a 7.77-second active Playwright submission and 69-second average gaps across four already prepared receipts. These observations point to supply, coordination, and reliability before click speed, but the missing stage trace prevents a precise causal breakdown.

At 100 receipts/hour the total budget is 36 seconds per new receipt. A provisional 100-role budget is 8 minutes of counted incremental discovery and duplicate checks, 17 minutes of form preparation and answer checks, 25 minutes of sequential submission and receipt verification, and 10 minutes for research, exceptions, and recovery. These are engineering budgets to test, not evidence of current performance. A 100-role test cannot silently start with a prefilled queue: acquisition and upkeep of its candidate reserve must be measured and charged to the campaign or reported separately as an amortized steady-state cost.

## Decision 1: profile-bound standing authorization

Introduce `StandingSubmissionPolicy` stored with the authenticated profile, with version, mode, enabled/revoked timestamps, allowed source/employer destination classes, existing eligibility constraints, daily/campaign caps, allowed answer classes, and owner provenance. An authenticated owner can opt in or revoke it. Merely starting a campaign does not create authorization. Existing `automatic` config remains an implementation mode; the new durable policy is the authority for campaign auto-submit. The default for profiles without a policy remains exact final-preview approval.

At intake, a covering policy may authorize preparation despite the mode's default `autoApply: false`; an uncovered request retains the existing `manual_policy` confirmation. At the pre-final boundary, evaluate the policy again against the current role, employer destination, complete answer plan, observed browser values, uploads, changed fields, eligibility, and today's caps. Write a versioned `AutoSubmissionDecision` with policy ID/version, role key, form fingerprint, decision, reason codes, and timestamp. Bind this decision to the final browser action. A material field or role change invalidates it and triggers a new decision. The agent and browser worker cannot create or widen a policy. Campaigns must stop forcing final approval when a policy is active, but direct links and ordinary requests use the same policy evaluator. Exact approval remains available when the owner chooses `always` or explicitly approves a held exception.

Current defaults cap global applications at 10 and full-time applications at 8 per day; campaign targets are at most 50. The owner policy must explicitly permit a higher daily ceiling, up to the current schema maximum of 100, before a 100-role live test. Count cap consumption at reserved final submission attempts rather than merely created or skipped application records, and release unused reservations on terminal non-submission. Two or more bounded campaign waves may share a single measured one-hour cohort; role reservations and cap accounting span waves. Do not silently raise caps or exceed site-specific limits to meet a speed metric.

The machine may auto-submit verified contact, resume, links, stable profile facts, and evidence-grounded company-specific prose. It must hold unknown facts, work authorization or other legal declarations not explicitly confirmed for reuse, compensation or availability outside confirmed preferences, account/OTP/CAPTCHA steps, human-authorship or AI-use restrictions, ambiguous destinations, changed final forms, and uncertain prior submit attempts. Optional unsupported fields stay blank only if the employer permits it. A hold records one precise question or takeover action and releases the lane. The owner should never be asked to reconfirm a fact already saved and valid for this scope.

On rollout, update the repository and installed job-application skill to reflect the owner-selected standing policy. The current skill's requirement for exact named approval remains the fallback when no standing policy covers the form. Skill text alone does not authorize submission; the server enforces the decision. Existing pending final approvals must not be auto-approved during migration.

## Decision 2: reliable one-lane execution

Keep one reusable application agent and one active external application tab. The server persists the queue, form snapshot, answer provenance, pre-final decision, attempt phase, and receipt. The worker inventories reachable steps, resolves deterministic aliases with field type and context, drafts only unresolved eligible prose in a bounded batch, fills values, reads them back after client-side persistence, and checks required/optional fields and validation messages. A final action is isolated from navigation buttons and executed at most once per attempt. The worker records an immediately durable `pre_final`/`final_action_started` phase boundary. Lost responses after the latter are fenced until employer-side outcome is reconciled.

Run a small, controlled browser comparison on the same Ashby and other ATS fixtures: existing headless Playwright, a supported regular-browser session, and only then one alternate runtime if the first two cannot meet reliability. Compare receipt rate, challenge rate, p50/p95 active time, and tokens. Do not disguise automation, solve CAPTCHAs, or treat anti-bot failure as permission to retry repeatedly. Route a human challenge to a held state and continue other roles. Reuse versioned adapter recipes only when origin and form fingerprint match; a mismatch returns to safe inventory and review.

## Decision 3: candidate supply and duplicate integrity

Maintain a bounded rolling reserve of suitable, unseen official employer postings. Prefer documented ATS public feeds and query adapters; use visible-browser sources with per-host request intervals, pagination limits, change detection, and 403/429/challenge stop behavior. Preserve listing URL, verified employer apply URL, ATS role ID, source, time, evidence, and eligibility decision. Never infer a usable employer destination from an aggregator link alone. Verify that a shortlisted role remains open immediately before preparation.

Deduplicate before queueing by stable ATS role IDs, normalized listing/apply URLs, and verified receipt destinations across sources and URL variants. The 23 September fix must be tested and deployed to production before automatic submission. A transactionally reserved role key prevents two queue entries or retries from reaching the same final action. Source health must distinguish zero listings, all handled, all ineligible, missing destination, parse drift, access restrictions, and source exhaustion; report coverage and yield so an empty pool is visible rather than mistaken for speed.

## Decision 4: instrumentation and acceptance

Use monotonic duration measurements plus UTC event timestamps for scan start/end, candidate screening, form load/inventory, deterministic fill, prose drafting, validation, review decision, owner hold, final click, receipt, recovery, and terminal outcome. Attribute worker CPU/browser time, model calls/tokens, agent/tool round trips, queue wait, owner wait, source fetches, failures, and blocked cases to campaign and application IDs. Keep private form content out of metrics. Report both wall-clock and active time per new verified receipt; attempted, already handled, rejected, blocked, and uncertain roles have separate counts. Unknown old measurements are unknown, not zero.

Release gates proceed in order: fixtures and policy regression; staging shadow decisions without final clicks; a small explicitly opted-in live cohort; 10 new receipts in six minutes; 25 in fifteen; then 100 in sixty. Each milestone counts the full fresh campaign plus attributable ongoing reserve maintenance, and reports supply shortages separately. Compare application fit and answer accuracy to the previous process. Stop and roll back automatic submission for any false receipt, duplicate final action, unconfirmed legal answer, or private-data leak. Origin-specific challenge or failure spikes disable that origin's automatic path without stopping healthy sources. A failed speed gate leaves the quality gates intact and produces a bottleneck trace, not a weaker eligibility rule.

## Migration and implementation order

1. Merge/deploy duplicate correction, add complete timing and source-yield traces, and capture a baseline.
2. Add policy schema/API and authenticated opt-in/revocation; implement shadow pre-final decisions and regression tests.
3. Remove campaign's unconditional `forceFinalApproval`, enforce standing-policy decisions in service and worker, and update both skill copies. Keep default exact approval.
4. Fix Ashby persisted-value and browser-environment failures with controlled comparative evidence.
5. Build the rolling candidate reserve and source diagnostics, then activate automatic lane for a small cohort.
6. Run staged live milestones; optimize only the measured largest remaining stage. Do not promote the one-hour claim before the 100-receipt gate.

## Risks and trade-offs

- **A standing policy submits an unsuitable role:** preserve hard eligibility rules, role/source evidence, a pre-final policy decision, daily caps, revocation, and audit; default to hold on ambiguity.
- **The form changes after planning:** compare observed values and form fingerprint immediately before final action; invalidate stale decisions.
- **A lost response causes duplicate submission:** persist final-action phase before the click, fence retries, and reconcile employer evidence before release.
- **A faster worker triggers employer blocks:** cap per-origin traffic, back off on 403/429/challenge, compare challenge rates, and keep a reliable human handoff.
- **The reserve hides preparation time:** charge reserve maintenance to campaign throughput and disclose preloaded-queue latency separately.
- **There are too few eligible jobs:** report the shortage and source diagnostics without relaxing fit or fabricating receipts.
